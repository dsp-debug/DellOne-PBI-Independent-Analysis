## UK Retail Sales Analysis (1996–2026)
## Topic Overview
This project provides a comprehensive evaluation of long-term consumer spending patterns in the United Kingdom over a 30-year period. It tracks the structural transformation of the retail economy, specifically focusing on the shift from traditional high-street shopping to digital channels, and the market's recovery and stabilization following the COVID-19 pandemic.

## Data Sources
The analysis is based on historical UK retail trade data (1996–2024) and projected market trends leading into 2026. Key metrics include:

* Total Sales Volume: Aggregate transaction values across all sectors.
* Sector-Specific Performance: Comparative data for Food, Clothing, Household Goods, and Automotive Fuel.
* Digital Metrics: Online sales revenue and total e-commerce market share.

## Key Insights

* The Pandemic Pivot: The 2020 lockdowns triggered a permanent structural shift. While total sales saw a sharp "Deep," online sales experienced a "Bump" that settled at a new, higher baseline rather than returning to pre-pandemic levels.
* Dominance of Food Retail: Food stores remain the most resilient "anchor" of the UK economy, currently valued at £0.88bn, providing stability during periods of inflation and volatility.
* Market Efficiency: The UK has achieved a 10.7% online sales share and maintains a steady 5.4% YoY growth rate, signaling a mature and healthy retail environment.
* Multichannel Future: Projections for 2026 suggest that long-term success depends on the integration of "Click and Collect" models and normalizing growth in a high-cost environment.
