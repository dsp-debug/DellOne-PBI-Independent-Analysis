This report evaluates a decade (2014-2024) of radiological data to assess the efficacy of current safety protocols and the long-term health risks to personnel. The analysis focuses on approximately 22,000 workers of which 11,000 workers had a measurable dose across the U.S nuclear security enterprise. This analysis specifically examines the relationship between workforce growth and individual exposure levels and identifies higher exposure locations. The objective is to provide data driven confirmation of safety compliance and to identify high activity zones requiring sustained oversight.

The data was obtained from Office of Environment, Health, Safety \& Security https://oriseapps.orau.gov/CER/REMSQueryTool focusing on 6 operational offices in the United states.



The key insights show that the Radiological safety program is performing very well and stable. While total radiation exposure appears high at 984,000, the average individual dose remains consistently below 100mrem with an average exposure of 59.12 mrem against a limit of 5,000 mrem, the risk of long-term health effects remains mathematically minimal. Exposure is concentrated in a few high activity sites and is primarily driven by Photon radiation. This indicates specific shielding improvements and site-specific interventions would result in greater safety for the workers. A complex radiological workload is being successfully managed while prioritizing the individual safety of the workers.
